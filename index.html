What is OCR, and when do I need it?

OCR stands for Optical Character Recognition. When a PDF is a scan (a picture of a document that came from a scanner or a phone camera), the text inside it is just pixels — your computer can't read it.

OCR looks at the picture, recognizes the letters and words, and adds an invisible text layer behind the image so you can search it with Ctrl+F, copy and paste from it, and screen readers can read it aloud.

Turn this on when: the PDF is a scanned legal doc, contract, signed page, fax, or anything that came from a copier. If you can't already select text by clicking and dragging across the page, you need OCR.

Leave it off when: the PDF was made digitally (Word export, Acrobat, designer's PDF) — it's already searchable, OCR would just waste time.
